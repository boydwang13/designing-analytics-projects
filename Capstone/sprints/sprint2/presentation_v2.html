3) Isolation Score: Components and Interpretation

Formula (v1.0):

Isolation_Score = 0.25*OutboundScarcity + 0.25*WeakWeightShare + 0.25*TargetConcentration + 0.25*WeakBridgeDeficit

Sub-metricInterpretationCore formula
OutboundScarcity Higher when a person rarely asks others for help 1 - out_degree/max_out_degree
WeakWeightShare Higher when outbound ties are mostly weak (low frequency) Weak_Tie_Outbound_Count / out_degree
TargetConcentration Higher when requests are concentrated on very few people Herfindahl：Σ(count_to_target/total_outbound)^2
WeakBridgeDeficit Higher when cross-team weak bridges are missing 1 - Weak_Cross_Team_Tie_Count/max(...)

All four components are normalized to [0,1]. Higher means higher isolation risk. Equal weights are used as the v1.0 baseline.

4) Team Density Heatmap (sprint2_team_density_heatmap.png)

How to read: Rows are source teams (who ask for help). Columns are target teams (who are asked). The diagonal is intra-team density; off-diagonal cells are cross-team density.

Unweighted directed team density (Cross & Parker block density)

Key findings in this snapshot

  • All six teams have intra/off-diagonal < 1 (DE=0.37, CS=0.97), so no classic silo team appears in this synthetic wave.
  • Analytics & BI acts as a net helper (higher inbound demand than outbound demand).
  • Data Engineering depends more on outside teams, especially Analytics & BI, than on internal ties.
  • This matrix turns visual structure into auditable directional dependency numbers.

8) PID Sprint 3 Tasks

From docs/PID.md, Sprint 3 (Weeks 5–6) focuses on predictive modeling:

  • Use isolation risk as binary supervised target y.
  • Use simulated employee attributes as features x (for example Seniority, Years_Exp, skill and team variables).
  • Train and tune models (for example Logistic Regression and Random Forest).
  • Use 80/20 split plus 5-fold cross-validation; evaluate AUC, Brier score, and confusion-matrix metrics.
